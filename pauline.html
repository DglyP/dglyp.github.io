Good to know

OCTOBER IN JAPAN

IC card

Get a Suica or Pasmo on arrival (Narita/Haneda, or any station machine) and tap it on every train, bus, and most konbini. Top up as you go — cash and card both work at the machines.

JR Pass or Hakone Free Pass

Price out the JR Pass against a Hakone Free Pass plus separate shinkansen tickets — for this route the JR Pass isn't always the cheaper option, since Hakone's local transport runs on its own pass.

Onsen etiquette

Wash and rinse fully before getting in. Tattoos can be an issue at some traditional ryokans — check ahead or look for a private/family bath option if that applies.

Weather

Early-to-mid October runs mild, roughly 16–24°C, with the odd rain shower. Layers plus one light rain shell cover it — no need for heavy jackets yet.

Cash still matters

Cards are more accepted than a few years ago, but small shops, temples, and some ramen counters are cash-only. Keep a stack of ¥1000 notes and coins on hand.

Konbini life

7-Eleven, Lawson, and FamilyMart are genuinely useful for meals, ATMs, and bathroom stops — don't skip them out of politeness, everyone uses them constantly.

Quick etiquette

No eating while walking, keep phone calls off trains, and carry a small bag for trash — public bins are rare. Shoes off wherever there's a raised entryway.

Bear awareness

Okutama has had active bear advisories. Carry a bear bell, stick to marked trails, and avoid hiking alone at dawn or dusk in that area.

Book lodges early

Kamikochi and the Kumano Kodo villages have very few rooms each, and October foliage season fills them fast. Lock these in before anything else on the route.
